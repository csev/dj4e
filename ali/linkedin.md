# The Campus-Linked Open Course

## A new space between a MOOC and university enrollment

For more than a decade, open online courses have made excellent educational material available around the world.

But access to lectures and assignments is not quite the same as taking part in a course.

Many self-paced learners begin with good intentions and then lose momentum. There may be no calendar, no active instructor, no office hours, and no sense that other learners are working through the material at the same time.

At the other extreme, a university course provides structure, support, classmates, instructional staff, and a course that is happening *right now*. But it also requires formal enrollment, significant tuition, and often academic credit that the learner may not need.

I think there is useful space between those two models.

I call it a **Campus-Linked Open Course**, or **CLOC**.

## What is a CLOC?

A CLOC is a non-credit online course linked to a university course while that course is actively being taught.

Online learners follow the same general calendar, receive regular course communication, and have access to selected office hours and instructional support.

The online and campus experiences are not identical, and we should not pretend that they are.

The goal is to combine the flexibility and accessibility of open online learning with some of the rhythm and human support of a live campus course.

## Why might this help?

A self-paced course gives learners tremendous freedom. But it also places nearly all responsibility for progress on the learner.

A CLOC adds a few simple things that may make a large difference:

* A calendar and suggested due dates
* Weekly communication
* Office hours and live help
* Access to instructional staff
* A cohort moving through the material together
* Continued access after the campus term ends

These features give learners a reason to keep going and somewhere to turn when they get stuck.

The course remains flexible. Online learners do not receive grades, take formal examinations, or lose points for late work. They can fall behind and continue after the campus semester ends.

This is not a cheaper imitation of university enrollment. It is a different educational experience for learners who want knowledge, support, and momentum but do not need academic credit.

## My first CLOC

My first CLOC is **Building Web Applications in Django**, linked to an undergraduate course at the University of Michigan School of Information.

The first offering begins August 1.

Online learners will receive early access, weekly communication, scheduled office hours, and selected help sessions with the instructional team. When the campus course begins, the online course will follow the same general sequence of topics.

The course includes readings, videos, programming exercises, Django assignments, automated feedback, and approximately 360 days of access.

The cost is **$300**. That fee supports the instructional staff who provide live help and respond to learners during the course.

CLOC learners are not formally enrolled at the University of Michigan. They do not receive university credit, grades, or a transcript.

What they receive is access to an active instructional process at a much lower cost than formal university enrollment.

## An experiment worth trying

This first offering is a pilot.

I want to learn whether the campus calendar helps online learners stay engaged, which forms of support are most useful, and how much instructional effort the model requires.

Learner feedback will be central to the experiment. When several people struggle with the same assignment or explanation, we can clarify instructions, add examples, or adjust help sessions while the course is still running.

The model also recognizes an important reality:

**Online content can be distributed almost for free. Human support cannot.**

A sustainable CLOC needs enough paid enrollment to support the people providing that help.

Universities already invest heavily in courses, instructional materials, faculty, and teaching assistants. The CLOC offers a way to extend some of that value beyond formally enrolled students without pretending that open participation is the same as university enrollment.

## A bridge into the active course

The first generation of online education focused on access to content.

The next step is better access to the educational process: structure, communication, feedback, and human help.

**A CLOC builds a bridge between flexible online learning and the living instructional community of an active campus course.**

It does not replace campus education, and it does not replace fully self-paced learning. It adds another option between the two.

For some learners, that may be exactly the right balance:

**Flexible but not isolated.
Structured but not punitive.
Supported but still affordable.**

My first CLOC will test that idea.
